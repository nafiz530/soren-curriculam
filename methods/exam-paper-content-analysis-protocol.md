# SSC/HSC Examination-Paper Content Analysis Protocol

**Status:** Proposed; freeze and timestamp the rubric before coding.  
**Purpose:** Test BEC-042 through public, already-administered exam papers. This is document analysis, not a student/teacher interview study.

## Research question
Across a transparent sample of SSC and HSC written papers, how are available marks distributed across cognitive-demand categories, and how does that vary by subject, board and year?

## Sampling frame
Target **48 papers**: exam years 2024 and 2025; Dhaka, Rajshahi and Chattogram boards; four papers at each level.
- **SSC:** Bangla 1st Paper, English 1st Paper, Mathematics, Science.
- **HSC:** Bangla 1st Paper, English 1st Paper, Physics, Biology.
- One administered paper per board × year × level × subject (3 × 2 × 2 × 4 = 48).

Verify that each paper was actually administered and that the version is authentic. Record missing cells and reasons; do not silently replace them. Flag common/identical papers and report both board-level sample size and unique paper forms. This purposive sample is not nationally representative. Record syllabus and assessment regime for each year.

## Traceability and unit
For every paper, record board, level, subject, year/session, paper code, syllabus regime, source URL, access date and authenticity caveat. Prefer official board sources; note any third-party archive. The unit is the smallest independently mark-bearing item or sub-item. Record maximum marks. Split independently answerable sub-items; otherwise code the dominant demand and flag mixed demand. Report mark-weighted shares as primary and item-count shares as secondary.

## Cognitive-demand rubric

| Code | Category | Operational rule |
|---|---|---|
| R | Recall / reproduction | Mainly remember or reproduce a fact, definition, formula, rule, passage or rehearsed procedure. |
| U | Understanding / explanation | Explain meaning, interpret information, summarise, classify or represent a familiar concept without substantial transfer. |
| A | Application | Select and apply a learned concept or procedure to a specified problem or context. Routine multi-step work can remain A. |
| AN | Analysis / evaluation | Non-routine reasoning, comparison, inference, evaluation of evidence, justification, or integration of multiple concepts. |
| C | Creation / investigation | Construct an original product, solution, model, question, experimental plan or investigation within task constraints. |

Do not classify from command verbs alone. A memorised “explain” can be R; a “calculate” task may be AN if method selection and non-routine reasoning are required. Cognitive demand is not the same as difficulty, student success, or curriculum importance. Code what the item requires as written.

## Additional item fields
Record item ID/page, marks, response format (MCQ, short response, extended response, essay, calculation, practical/data interpretation, other), content strand, real-world context (yes/no), evidence/justification required (yes/no), optional-choice status, cognitive-demand code, mixed-demand flag and a one-sentence rationale.

## Procedure
1. Inventory candidate papers and log missing, duplicate, uncertain and non-comparable papers.
2. Pilot the rubric on two papers if feasible; revise and freeze/date the codebook before coding the sample.
3. Code each mark-bearing sub-item with a source location and short rationale.
4. Re-code a random 20% after at least two weeks without viewing original codes. If a second trained coder is available, independently double-code 20% and report the agreement statistic. If only one coder is available, report intra-rater stability, not inter-rater reliability.
5. Retain original codes and adjudication notes; apply the written rubric consistently.
6. Report mark-weighted and item-count distributions by level, subject, board and year. Record denominators and missing data.
7. Sensitivity checks: exclude common/duplicate papers; treat mixed-demand items conservatively; compare years separately where syllabus regimes differ.

## Interpretation
Do not equate a high share of R items with proof that students only memorise, or infer classroom teaching directly from paper content. Papers show assessment demands; claims about teaching, tutoring and actual learning require separate evidence. Compare cautiously with Sultana & Rahman (2018; SRC-077), which analysed 16 purposively selected SSC General Science papers from 2015/2016 and interviewed 48 students. That study is historical and single-subject, not proof of current system-wide patterns.

## Ethics and limitations
This design does not recruit human participants, avoiding an interview/survey component. Still follow institutional requirements, document provenance, respect copyright and do not publish sensitive unreleased materials. Store item IDs/codes instead of republishing full papers where appropriate. The sample covers selected subjects, boards and years only; describe it as bounded content analysis, not a census of Bangladesh exams.

## Pre-coding checklist
- [ ] Sample, years, boards and subjects frozen.
- [ ] Source URLs, access dates and authenticity checked.
- [ ] Syllabus/assessment regime recorded.
- [ ] Rubric piloted and versioned before full coding.
- [ ] Mark-weighting and unit-of-analysis rules applied consistently.
- [ ] Duplicates and missing cells documented.
- [ ] Agreement/stability procedure chosen before coding.
- [ ] Claims limited to the sampled papers.
