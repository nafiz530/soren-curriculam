# SRC-049 — Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques

- **Authors:** John Dunlosky, Katherine A. Rawson, Elizabeth J. Marsh, Mitchell J. Nathan, Daniel T. Willingham
- **Journal:** *Psychological Science in the Public Interest*, 14(1), 4–58
- **Publication:** 2013
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266
- **Source:** https://doi.org/10.1177/1529100612453266
- **Language:** English
- **Type:** Review of ten learning techniques
- **Relevant claims:** Relative utility of practice testing, distributed practice, self-explanation, interleaving, rereading, highlighting, and other techniques.
- **Reported synthesis:** Practice testing and distributed practice received high-utility ratings; several other techniques had moderate or low ratings because evidence generalizability or conditions were limited.
- **Appraisal:** Influential review that explicitly compares techniques and generalizability. Ratings reflect evidence available to the authors in 2013 and should be updated with later reviews and classroom evidence.
- **Limits:** Not a full teaching philosophy; effects depend on content, implementation, learner, and criterion task. Do not infer that low-rated techniques never help or that practice testing alone guarantees deep transfer.
- **Status:** Identified; technique-by-technique appraisal and update search pending.
