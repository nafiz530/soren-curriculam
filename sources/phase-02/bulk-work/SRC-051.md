# SRC-051 — Active Learning Increases Student Performance in STEM

- **Authors:** Scott Freeman, Sarah L. Eddy, Miles McDonough, Michelle K. Smith, Nnadozie Okoroafor, Hannah Jordt, Mary Pat Wenderoth
- **Journal:** *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 111(23), 8410–8415
- **Publication:** 2014
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1319030111
- **URL:** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1319030111
- **Language:** English
- **Type:** Meta-analysis
- **Population / setting:** Undergraduate science, technology, engineering, and mathematics courses.
- **Reported synthesis:** Across 225 studies, active learning improved average examination/concept-inventory performance by 0.47 SD; failure odds were higher under traditional lecturing (reported odds ratio 1.95).
- **Appraisal:** Large and influential synthesis for undergraduate STEM teaching. The outcome domain and population must remain explicit.
- **Limits:** Do not transport numerical effects directly to Bangladeshi secondary schools, humanities, all forms of lecture, or all active-learning designs. Active learning is a broad category and quality varies.
- **Status:** Identified; underlying study heterogeneity and risk-of-bias review pending.
