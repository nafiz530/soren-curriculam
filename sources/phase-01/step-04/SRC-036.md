# SRC-036 — Ali & Hamid, Teaching English to the Test in Bangladesh

**Title:** “Teaching English to the Test: Why Does Negative Washback Exist within Secondary Education in Bangladesh?”  
**Authors:** Md. Maksud Ali and M. Obaidul Hamid  
**Journal:** *Language Assessment Quarterly*, 17(2), 129–146  
**Published online:** 2020-01-27  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1080/15434303.2020.1717495  
**Type:** Peer-reviewed journal article  
**Status:** Referenced; publisher abstract reviewed

## Research design and reported argument

The article reports interviews with 12 secondary-school teachers, supplemented by policy documents, media materials, and NGO reports. Its central argument is that negative washback cannot be explained by test design alone: wider socio-political, economic, social-psychological, and accountability conditions may shape teachers' choices alongside assessment.

## Use in this project

Use as a counterweight to a simple claim that examinations mechanically determine classroom teaching. It suggests a broader mechanism model: test design interacts with incentives, school and family expectations, institutional accountability, teacher knowledge, and context.

## Appraisal and limitations

- The 12-teacher interview sample supports contextual interpretation, not prevalence estimates.
- The abstract describes supplementary documentary material but does not by itself establish how systematically each document was selected or appraised; inspect the full text before relying on detailed causal claims.
- The article concerns secondary English teaching in Bangladesh; generalization to other subjects or educational stages needs separate evidence.
- The article does not prove that test reform alone is ineffective; it warns against single-cause explanations.
