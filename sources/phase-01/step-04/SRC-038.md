# SRC-038 — Islam, Hoque & Hoque, Inequalities in Private Tutoring of English

**Title:** “Inequalities in Private Tutoring of English: A Phenomenological Qualitative Study Based on Bangladeshi Higher Secondary Students”  
**Authors:** M. Monjurul Islam, Shams Hoque, and Kazi Enamul Hoque  
**Journal:** *The Qualitative Report*, 26(3), 734–750  
**Publication year:** 2021  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.46743/2160-3715/2021.4429  
**Type:** Peer-reviewed qualitative study  
**Status:** Referenced; article metadata and abstract reviewed

## Research design and reported finding

The study uses in-depth semi-structured interviews with 11 Bangladeshi higher-secondary students to explore their experiences of private English tutoring. The authors report themes involving unequal access, financial capability, and social expectations around tutoring.

## Use in this project

Use as qualitative evidence about how students experience tutoring-related inequality and social pressure. It can help identify possible mechanisms and questions for further study; it cannot estimate how common those experiences are among all students.

## Appraisal and limitations

- The sample is small and designed for depth, not statistical representativeness.
- The study focuses on English tutoring and higher-secondary students.
- Participant accounts are important evidence about experience but should not be treated as independent verification of system-wide prevalence or causal effects.
- Avoid reducing tutoring to harm alone: tutoring may provide additional explanation or support for some learners, while unequal access and exam-oriented incentives may create other problems.
