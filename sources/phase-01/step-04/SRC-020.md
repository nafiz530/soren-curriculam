# SRC-020 — PRISMA 2020 Statement

**Title:** The PRISMA 2020 statement: an updated guideline for reporting systematic reviews  
**Authors:** Matthew J. Page et al.  
**Publication:** BMJ, 2021  
**Source type:** Methodological reporting guideline and explanatory article  
**Primary URL:** https://www.bmj.com/content/372/bmj.n71  
**Official checklist:** https://www.prisma-statement.org/prisma-2020-checklist

## Main contribution

PRISMA 2020 provides an updated 27-item reporting checklist, an expanded explanation, and flow diagrams intended to improve transparent reporting of systematic reviews. It asks reviewers to report why the review was undertaken, what methods were used, and what was found, including information about sources, search strategies, selection, and synthesis.

## Intended use in this project

Use PRISMA as a reference for transparent reporting if a later topic receives a formal systematic review. Its emphasis on explicit questions, search sources, criteria, and reporting also informs the broader project protocol.

## Limits and cautions

- PRISMA is a reporting guideline, not a tool that independently grades source quality.
- It does not prove a review is exhaustive or methodologically sound merely because a checklist is completed.
- Its primary scope concerns systematic reviews, particularly those evaluating health interventions; not every item fits conceptual, philosophical, historical, or policy analysis.
- The project must not describe itself as a systematic review unless its actual search, screening, appraisal, and synthesis procedures justify that label.

## Claims this source can support

- PRISMA 2020 includes a 27-item checklist and updated flow diagrams.
- Transparent reporting of rationale, methods, and findings is a central purpose of the guideline.

## Claims it cannot support by itself

- That a source is credible or that a particular education intervention works.
- That the present project has conducted a systematic review.
- That all forms of educational research should use one identical method.

## Appraisal note

The source is a peer-reviewed methodological article and is linked to the official PRISMA checklist. Its use here is narrow: reporting transparency, not universal source ranking.
