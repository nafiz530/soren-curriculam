# SRC-023 — American Educational Research Association Code of Ethics

**Publisher:** American Educational Research Association (AERA)  
**Approval:** AERA Council, February 2011  
**Source type:** Professional research ethics code  
**Official page:** https://www.aera.net/about-aera/aera-rules-policies/professional-ethics  
**Code PDF:** https://www.aera.net/portals/38/docs/About_AERA/CodeOfEthics%281%29.PDF

## Main contribution

AERA's Code of Ethics articulates principles and standards for educational research, including professional competence, integrity, scholarly responsibility, respect for people's rights and dignity, and social responsibility. The code emphasizes the welfare and protection of people and groups with whom researchers work.

## Intended use in this project

Use it as a reference for research integrity, fair representation of sources, respect for participants, and careful consideration before any future primary data collection involving students, teachers, families, or other people.

## Limits and cautions

- It is professional guidance from a US educational research association, not Bangladesh law.
- It does not replace local school permissions, guardian consent, participant consent, ethics review, data-protection requirements, or safeguarding procedures.
- Ethical responsibilities depend on the actual design and context; a general code does not resolve every case automatically.

## Claims this source can support

- AERA's professional code includes principles concerning competence, integrity, research responsibility, rights and dignity, and social responsibility.
- Research involving people requires ethical consideration rather than treating data collection as a purely technical activity.

## Claims it cannot support by itself

- That a particular research design has received ethical approval.
- That the project meets all Bangladesh legal or institutional requirements.
- That the same consent or approval procedure applies to every study.

## Appraisal note

The official AERA page and linked code are primary sources for the association's own ethical standards. They inform the project protocol but must be supplemented by local requirements before primary data collection.
