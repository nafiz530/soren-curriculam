# SRC-039 — Asian Development Bank, Education without Formal Schooling through Tablets and Tutors

**Title:** “Education without Formal Schooling through Tablets and Tutors: Evidence from Out-of-School Children in Bangladesh during the COVID-19 Pandemic”  
**Publisher:** Asian Development Bank  
**Publication year:** 2024  
**Type:** Research report / intervention evaluation  
**Status:** Referenced; publisher summary reviewed

## Design and reported finding

The report studies an intervention for out-of-school children in Bangladesh during the COVID-19 pandemic that combined tablets and private tutoring. The publisher summary reports positive learning-score effects of approximately 0.25 standard deviations in mathematics and 0.17 standard deviations in Bangla.

## Use in this project

Use as a counterexample to any blanket claim that tutoring is inherently ineffective or harmful. It suggests that additional instruction may help in a specific intervention and population. The treatment combined tablets and tutoring, so the separate contribution of each component must not be assumed without examining the study design.

## Appraisal and limitations

- The population was out-of-school children during the pandemic, not a representative sample of mainstream secondary students preparing for SSC/HSC.
- The intervention combined components; the reported effects cannot automatically be attributed to tutoring alone.
- This study does not directly test exam washback, credential competition, or the net effects of routine fee-based tutoring in mainstream schools.
- Inspect the full report for allocation, attrition, outcome measures, implementation, uncertainty intervals, and subgroup analyses before using its estimates in a major recommendation.
