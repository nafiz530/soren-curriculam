# SRC-042 — Fred Hirsch, Social Limits to Growth

**Title:** *Social Limits to Growth*  
**Author:** Fred Hirsch  
**Publisher/year:** Harvard University Press, 1976  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.4159/harvard.9780674497900  
**Type:** Scholarly book in political economy  
**Status:** Referenced; publisher metadata and relevant chapter descriptions reviewed

## Core argument relevant to this project

Hirsch distinguishes ordinary material goods from positional goods, whose value depends partly on relative scarcity, status, or access. Education and access to desirable jobs can acquire positional features when their value depends on being ahead of others or gaining access to opportunities that cannot be expanded equally for everyone. In such settings, individually rational investment in credentials can intensify competition without allowing every participant to secure the same relative advantage.

## Use in this project

Use Hirsch to develop the positional-competition mechanism: when a limited number of seats or jobs carry high rewards, improving one's relative rank can matter even if absolute learning gains are small. This helps explain why families might increase exam preparation and tutoring when others do the same. Test the mechanism against Bangladesh-specific evidence about admissions, school reputation, employment, and credential requirements.

## Appraisal and limitations

- The book provides a conceptual political-economy argument, not a direct empirical test of Bangladesh's education system.
- Positional competition is not the only possible explanation for educational investment; learning, intrinsic value, professional competence, and genuine skill needs may also matter.
- The framework does not prove that all educational goods are positional or that expanding educational access is futile.
- The exact claims used in the final synthesis must be tied to relevant chapters/pages after closer reading.
