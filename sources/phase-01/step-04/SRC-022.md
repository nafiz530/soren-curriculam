**Latest update identified:** 15 May 2026

## Main contribution

The Magenta Book provides guidance on scoping, designing, conducting, using, and communicating evaluation. It distinguishes process, impact, and value-for-money evaluation. Its methods annex describes theory-based, experimental, quasi-experimental, synthesis, and other evaluation approaches. It emphasizes choosing methods that fit the question and examining how context and mechanisms affect outcomes.

## Intended use in this project

Use its guidance to clarify causal questions, alternative explanations, implementation, context, and the distinction between whether a programme worked, how it was delivered, for whom it worked, and at what cost.

## Limits and cautions

- It is UK government guidance, not Bangladesh law or a binding requirement for this project.
- The framework does not prove that any specific intervention works.
- Method selection must account for the question, feasible data, ethics, and local context.
- Theory-based evaluation can investigate causal mechanisms, but the credibility of conclusions depends on the quality and specificity of evidence and the treatment of alternative explanations.

## Claims this source can support

- The 2026 guidance distinguishes different evaluation purposes and discusses a range of methods.
- Credible causal reasoning requires testing theories and considering alternative causes, not simply observing change after an intervention.

## Claims it cannot support by itself

- That a particular reform caused an observed outcome.
- That UK evaluation procedures transfer unchanged to Bangladesh.
- That every project needs the same evaluation design.

## Appraisal note

The current official guidance is useful for evaluation concepts and is date-stamped. Its institutional origin and national context must remain explicit when applying it to this research.
