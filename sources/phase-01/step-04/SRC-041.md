# SRC-041 — Randall Collins, The Credential Society

**Title:** *The Credential Society: An Historical Sociology of Education and Stratification*  
**Author:** Randall Collins  
**Publisher/year:** Academic Press, 1979  
**Type:** Scholarly monograph in historical sociology  
**Status:** Referenced; bibliographic records and book metadata reviewed

## Core argument relevant to this project

Collins examines the historical relationship between educational credentials, occupational stratification, organizational power, and social status. His credentialist account challenges a simple human-capital explanation in which more education automatically reflects or produces more productive skill. It directs attention to how educational qualifications become requirements for access to occupational positions and how groups may use credentials to regulate entry and protect status.

## Use in this project

Use Collins to formulate questions about whether qualifications are rewarded because they represent job-relevant learning, because organizations use them as convenient screening devices, or because credential requirements reflect competition among groups and occupations. Compare these explanations with Spence's signaling model and Dore's analysis of qualification competition.

## Appraisal and limitations

- This is a theoretical and historical sociological argument, not direct evidence about contemporary Bangladesh.
- It does not imply that education never builds useful skills or that all qualification requirements are illegitimate.
- Its arguments must be translated into testable, locally grounded questions about hiring, admissions, credential requirements, and actual competence.
- The source record establishes the book's identity and relevance; detailed claims should be tied to specific chapters/pages when the book is consulted.
