# SRC-035 — Rahman et al., Washback of Assessment on English Teaching-Learning Practice at Secondary Schools

**Title:** “Washback of assessment on English teaching-learning practice at secondary schools”  
**Authors:** Kh. Atikur Rahman, Prodhan Mahbub Ibna Seraj, Md. Kamrul Hasan, Ehsan Namaziandost, and co-authors  
**Journal:** *Language Testing in Asia*, 11, article 12  
**Publication date:** 2021-07-01  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1186/s40468-021-00129-2  
**Type:** Peer-reviewed open-access empirical article  
**Status:** Referenced; article page and abstract reviewed

## Research design and reported finding

The study investigates how Bangladesh's Secondary School Certificate (SSC) English examinations relate to English teaching and learning. The authors report a qualitative design using teacher interviews, student focus-group discussions, and classroom observations across 12 secondary schools. Schools were selected by convenience sampling. The authors report examination-oriented teaching and learning and identify perceived curriculum–test mismatch, teacher understanding, attitudes, and pressure from schools and parents as contributing factors.

## Use in this project

Use as direct but bounded evidence about perceived SSC English washback in the sampled schools. It can inform mechanisms connecting high-stakes assessment to classroom practice, not a national prevalence estimate or a causal estimate for all subjects.

## Appraisal and limitations

- The study is qualitative and context-specific; its strength is mechanism and experience, not national representativeness.
- Convenience sampling limits transferability.
- Interviews, focus groups, and observations offer methodological variety, but the degree of independence among sources and the handling of contradictory cases must be checked in the full text.
- The reported findings do not establish that every examination causes negative washback or that removing examinations would improve learning.
- The study concerns SSC English, not all SSC/HSC subjects or every use of examinations.
