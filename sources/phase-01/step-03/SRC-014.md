# SRC-014 — Michael Spence, Job Market Signaling

**Title:** “Job Market Signaling”  
**Author:** Michael Spence  
**Journal:** *The Quarterly Journal of Economics*, Volume 87, Issue 3, August 1973, pages 355–374  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.2307/1882010  
**Type:** Peer-reviewed journal article  
**Status:** Referenced; canonical bibliographic record identified

## Step 3 relevance

Spence develops a model of signaling in labor markets where employers face uncertainty about applicants' productive capabilities. Observable attributes can influence decisions when direct information about future performance is unavailable. Educational qualifications can operate as one such signal.

## Use

Use this model to explain one reason employers and institutions may value credentials. Keep signaling distinct from learning, certification, and selection, even though these functions can overlap.

## Caution

A theoretical model does not establish that every credential is accurate, fair, or efficient. It does not prove that education exists only to signal ability or that qualifications have no learning value.
