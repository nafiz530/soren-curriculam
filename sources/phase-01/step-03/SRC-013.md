**Status:** Referenced; open-access article identified

## Step 3 relevance

Biesta develops three domains for thinking about educational purpose:

- **Qualification:** knowledge, skills, understandings, and capacities that enable people to do things.
- **Socialization:** becoming part of social, cultural, and political orders and practices.
- **Subjectification:** learners' development as subjects of their own lives, including freedom and responsible agency.

The framework helps identify dimensions that may coexist or conflict. It is directly relevant to the draft's categories of knowledge, culture, citizenship, agency, and certification.

## Use

Use the framework as an analytical lens and compare it with the chapter's working categories. Ask what the framework clarifies, what it leaves less visible, and how it handles the distinction between acquiring qualifications and the social logic of qualification.

## Caution

This is a philosophical framework, not an empirical ranking or a universally accepted definition of education's purposes. Subjectification is debated and should not be reduced to individual preference or unrestricted choice.
