# EV-004-002 — Preliminary Evidence Synthesis: Credential Competition and Examination Pressure in Bangladesh

**Status:** Draft evidence note; not a final conclusion  
**Date:** 2026-10-09  
**Question:** Why does examination pressure persist in Bangladesh, and what roles do credential competition, selection, signaling, and tutoring play?

## Preliminary synthesis

The current sources suggest that examination pressure should be examined as an interaction among test design, institutional accountability, family expectations, scarce opportunities, credential use, and supplementary tutoring. The SSC English studies provide context-specific evidence of examination-oriented practices and point to wider pressures. Dore, Collins, Hirsch, and Spence provide distinct theoretical mechanisms for credential competition, occupational credentialism, positional scarcity, and signaling, but their frameworks cannot substitute for contemporary Bangladesh-specific evidence. Al Amin and Greenwood's 2018 mixed-methods study adds broader local evidence about the social system sustaining examination pressure, though it remains focused on English and does not identify national causal effects. Tutoring studies indicate both perceived access inequality and a credible possibility of instructional benefit under a targeted intervention.

The evidence is heterogeneous and should not be pooled into a single effect estimate. Samples, constructs, populations, and designs differ. The current source set does not justify a national causal conclusion or a claim that exams are the sole or main cause of poor learning.

## Evidence classification

- **Established evidence:** none yet for a broad national claim. The Step 4 threshold requires close claim fit and an active counterevidence search; full-text appraisal and broader search remain incomplete.
- **Bounded empirical findings:** negative SSC English washback was reported in sampled settings; tutoring-related inequality was described by interviewed higher-secondary students.
- **Theoretical mechanisms:** signaling under information asymmetry (Spence); credential competition/screening (Dore).
- **Counterevidence / qualification:** the ADB intervention is inconsistent with the blanket proposition that tutoring cannot improve learning, but it does not settle the effects of routine tutoring in mainstream schools.
- **Open hypothesis:** examination pressure persists partly because results help allocate scarce opportunities and multiple institutions and families respond to the same credential incentives.
- **Important evidence gap:** the selected sources do not measure the full preparation-for-the-future construct.
