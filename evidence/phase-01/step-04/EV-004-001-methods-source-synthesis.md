# EV-004-001 — Methodological Source Synthesis for Step 4

**Status:** Methodological evidence note; approved for use in the working protocol  
**Research step:** Phase 1, Step 4 — Research Methodology & Evidence Standards  
**Related records:** SRC-020–SRC-034; `methods/step-04-evidence-synthesis-protocol.md`  
**Important boundary:** This note synthesizes guidance about research methods. It is not evidence that a particular education policy or intervention works.

## Synthesis question

Which methods and appraisal sources can support a transparent, claim-sensitive evidence synthesis without pretending that one review framework, checklist, or study hierarchy is suitable for every question?

## Source-derived contributions

| Source IDs | Methodological contribution | Intended use | Limit or caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| SRC-020 | PRISMA 2020 provides a structured reporting checklist and flow diagrams for systematic reviews. | Reporting searches, selection, and synthesis when a formal systematic review is actually conducted. | Reporting guidance does not itself establish source quality, completeness, or causal validity. |
| SRC-021 | WWC Version 5.0 gives education-intervention appraisal procedures within a defined remit. | Informing appraisal of eligible education intervention studies. | Not a universal hierarchy for philosophy, history, qualitative experience, or Bangladesh policy documents. |
| SRC-022 | The 2026 Magenta Book distinguishes evaluation purposes and describes theory-based, experimental/quasi-experimental, synthesis, and other methods. | Matching evaluation method to question and examining implementation, causal alternatives, context, and cost. | UK public-sector guidance; does not prove intervention effectiveness or transferability to Bangladesh. |
| SRC-024–026 | Cartwright and Hardie, Pawson, and Pawson and Tilley emphasize causal conditions, mechanisms, and context. | Asking what works, for whom, under what circumstances, and what must be present for an effect to travel. | Frameworks guide reasoning; the quality of conclusions still depends on underlying evidence. |
| SRC-027–029 | Slavin, Popay et al., and Gough, Oliver and Thomas discuss best-evidence, narrative synthesis, and systematic-review methods. | Choosing transparent synthesis approaches when study quality, constructs, or settings vary. | No approach removes the need to document selection, appraisal, and interpretation. |
| SRC-030–033 | CASP, MMAT, ROBIS, and AMSTAR 2 provide structured appraisal prompts for different study/review types. | Selecting a design-appropriate tool and recording methodological concerns. | These tools have different intended scopes and are not interchangeable universal quality scores. |
| SRC-034 | Koretz examines what educational test scores can and cannot show. | Checking whether an exam score validly supports a claim about broad learning or future readiness. | Does not imply that all examinations are invalid or harmful. |
| SRC-023 | AERA's ethics code articulates professional responsibilities and respect for rights and dignity. | Ethical orientation for research integrity and participant protection. | Does not replace Bangladesh law, institutional review, or school/guardian permissions. |

## Methodological conclusion

The sources support a **plural, question-sensitive method** rather than one universal evidence ladder. A formal systematic review is appropriate only when its question, search, screening, appraisal, and synthesis procedures can actually be implemented and reported. Other questions may require conceptual analysis, primary-document analysis, structured narrative synthesis, realist reasoning, or mixed methods.

The protocol therefore adopts four safeguards:
1. define constructs before comparing studies;
2. appraise each source relative to the claim it is asked to support;
3. record independent evidence, counterevidence, sponsorship, language, and context;
4. connect load-bearing claims and major recommendations to traceable repository artifacts and an adversarial review.

## What this note does not establish

- It does not show whether examination-focused education helps or harms students.
- It does not determine which educational purposes should take priority.
- It does not establish that a given international intervention will work in Bangladesh.
- It does not mean the project has conducted a systematic review.

## Application requirement

For every substantive research question, select the relevant parts of the protocol, record why they fit, and preserve a search log, evidence table, review log, and decision record when the claim is load-bearing.
