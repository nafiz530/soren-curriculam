# Evidence-to-Claim Matrix — Manuscript Preparation Version

**Status:** Working draft; not a systematic review and not yet approved for publication.  
**Purpose:** Trace each substantive claim to source IDs and manuscript sections.  
**Evidence grades:** *Supported* means credible evidence directly supports the claim within the stated scope; *plausible* means mechanisms or limited studies support it but coverage/causal identification is incomplete; *contested* means credible evidence conflicts or interpretation materially differs; *unknown* means evidence is absent, unidentified or insufficient. These are claim-relative qualitative grades, not a formal GRADE assessment. Normative claims and method rules are explicitly marked as not empirically graded.

## Method and design rules (not empirical claims)

- **Rule 1 — Traceability (formerly BEC-028):** Every load-bearing manuscript statement must trace through a BEC claim to a registered SRC and, where available, a primary source or archived artifact. Record unresolved provenance explicitly.
- **Rule 2 — Claim-relative appraisal:** Grade evidence for the specific claim, design, population and outcome; do not use one study-quality label as a universal grade.
- **Rule 3 — Separation of categories:** Keep empirical claims, conceptual interpretations, normative commitments, design hypotheses and methods requirements distinct.
- **Rule 4 — Feasible design (formerly BEC-027):** A proposed reform must specify costs, staff capacity, low-resource alternatives and failure criteria before national scaling.
- **Rule 5 — Publication gate:** The review is a structured narrative review, not a systematic review, until search, screening, appraisal and reporting meet the approved Step 4 protocol.
