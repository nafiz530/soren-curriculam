## Working title

**Exam-Oriented or Future-Ready? An Evidence Review of Learning Outcomes, Assessment Incentives and Reform Design in Bangladesh's School System**

## Non-negotiable reader-facing citation rule

The manuscript must be independently understandable. **Never write repository identifiers such as `SRC-037`, `BEC-036`, or file-path references in the paper's prose, tables, captions, footnotes, or reference list.** Those identifiers belong only in the internal research repository.

Use a consistent author–date citation style (APA 7 is the default unless a target journal requires another style): for example, *(Directorate of Primary Education, 2023)*, *(Mahmud, [verified publication year])* or *(Campaign for Popular Education [CAMPE], 2024)*. Give a complete reference entry for every cited work, including title, issuing institution/journal, year, volume/issue/pages where applicable, DOI or direct stable URL, and access date for dynamic pages where appropriate. Never guess a missing author, year, title, journal, page range, DOI or URL. Resolve missing metadata before submission. A reference list entry must match the exact source actually used. Direct links to primary reports are preferred; news reports should be cited only when the original study/report is unavailable and the limitation is stated.

Internal source IDs may appear in the source register, claims matrix, appraisal sheets and extraction files only.

## Paper type and claim discipline

This is a **structured narrative evidence review with realist-informed reasoning**, not a systematic review and not a meta-analysis. Do not call the search exhaustive, systematic or PRISMA-compliant. The paper must distinguish:
- **Descriptive findings:** what a report or dataset measured.
- **Associations:** variables that co-vary without proving causation.
- **Causal findings:** only where design and assumptions support them.
- **Mechanisms:** plausible pathways (e.g., high-stakes selection affecting teaching incentives) whose strength and reach require local evidence.
- **Normative judgements:** explicit values and trade-offs, not empirical facts.
- **Design proposals:** testable recommendations, not established findings.

Use calibrated wording: “the report found,” “is associated with,” “is consistent with,” “may contribute,” “the evidence located does not establish,” and “requires testing.” Do not write “proves,” “causes,” “all students,” “the entire system,” or “Bangladesh's education system only teaches to exams” unless the evidence truly supports that scope.

## Research questions

1. What do the best available data show about foundational learning in Bangladesh, and how do outcomes differ by grade, subject, gender, region, school type and socioeconomic context?
2. What evidence links examination incentives, washback, tutoring, guidebooks and credential competition to teaching and learning?
3. What do Bangladesh's education policies and curricula intend, and what is known—and not known—about implementation?
4. Which learning-science findings and international policy experiences may be relevant to Bangladesh, under what conditions, and with what transfer risks?
5. What reform framework, pilot design and evaluation thresholds follow from the evidence?

## Recommended manuscript architecture (target 9,000–11,000 words before references/appendices)

1. **Abstract (200–250 words).** Context, objective, review approach, strongest findings, evidence limitations and practical conclusion. Write last.
2. **Introduction (800–1,000).** The question and why it matters; Bangladesh context; avoid assuming the answer; contribution and research questions.
3. **Conceptual framework (700–900).** Distinguish learning, certification, selection, credential signalling and broader future capabilities. Define “exam-oriented” and “future-ready” as dimensions that can coexist rather than a simplistic binary.
4. **Methods (900–1,200).** Structured narrative search; search dates and English/Bangla terms; source types; screening and inclusion/exclusion; appraisal approach; extraction and evidence grading; limits. Report the real search process, not a fabricated systematic flow.
5. **Bangladesh policy and system context (700–900).** Policy aims, curriculum and assessment changes, financing, institutional variation and implementation evidence. Distinguish formal intent from classroom reality.
6. **Foundational learning outcomes (1,000–1,300).** Primary assessment, learning poverty, comparability caveats, gender/region/school-type variation and limits of available indicators.
7. **Examination incentives, tutoring and credential competition (1,200–1,500).** Washback studies, tutoring/guidebook prevalence and spending, family strategies, stress evidence, credential competition and counterarguments. Do not treat tutoring as uniformly harmful or infer national prevalence from local/subject-specific studies.
8. **Curriculum, pedagogy and teacher support (700–900).** What is supported about retrieval, spacing, feedback, active learning and differentiated instruction; distinguish transfer from international studies to Bangladeshi classrooms.
9. **Equity, inclusion and wellbeing (600–800).** Gender, geography, disability, household income, language, digital access and assessment burden; explicitly identify data gaps.
10. **Comparative lessons and transferability (700–900).** Carefully selected systems, policy mechanisms, implementation conditions and contextual limits. Avoid ranking countries or attributing outcomes to one policy based on PISA alone.
11. **Synthesis: what the evidence does and does not say (600–800).** Answer each research question with evidence grades and counterevidence; separate supported conclusions from plausible mechanisms and unknowns.
12. **Proposed reform framework (700–900).** A layered model aligning foundational mastery, subject knowledge, application, reasoning, communication, collaboration, agency and civic/ethical judgement. Do not imply a competency list is itself validated.
13. **Implementation and evaluation plan (700–900).** Staged pilot, teacher preparation, material and assessment alignment, cost, equity safeguards, monitoring, independent evaluation, stopping/adaptation rules.
14. **Limitations and conclusion (400–600).** Search coverage, source quality, measurement limits, missing causal evidence, authorship and next evidence needs.
15. **References and appendices.** Complete author–date references; search log; appraisal summary; extraction tables; item-coding rubric; supplementary charts and sensitivity notes.

## Core verified Bangladesh data for the paper

The Directorate of Primary Education's *National Student Assessment 2022 in Bangladesh: Grades 3 and 5* defines grade-level competency as the **Proficient** and **Advanced** performance levels. The official report's executive summary and detailed tables report:

| Subject | Grade | 2017 | 2022 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Bangla | 3 | 47% | 51% | +4 percentage points |
| Bangla | 5 | 44% | 50% | +6 percentage points |
| Mathematics | 3 | 34% | 39% | +5 percentage points |
| Mathematics | 5 | 32% | 30% | −2 percentage points |

Government of Bangladesh. https://objectstorage.ap-dcc-gazipur-1.oraclecloud15.com/n/axvjbnqprylg/b/V2Ministry/o/office-dpe/2024/12/468747c2128a447583c707fb5ddf274b.pdf. Official publication listing: https://dpe.gov.bd/site/publications/8fedb608-0b49-4935-bf2d-9e15a3b66525/NSA-2022-Final-Report

**Interpretation caveat:** the 2022 assessment added pre-reading and early-numeracy items and included geographical type as an additional stratification variable. Report comparisons as the assessment report presents them, with this methodological context. Do not equate these percentages with a universal measure of all reading or mathematics ability.

The World Bank's *Bangladesh Learning Poverty Brief* (2024 brief) reports 51% learning poverty (boys 54%, girls 49%), 50% learning deprivation (boys 53%, girls 48%), and 2.4% schooling deprivation; the brief says the 51% estimate reflects data from during COVID school closures. Treat this as a dated, brief-specific estimate, not a 2026 current estimate. The separate *Learning Poverty Global Database* workbook is a 2021 update with data from 2001–2019; its reported Bangladesh values (2015: 57.2%; 2017: 58.1%) must **not** be plotted as a continuous trend against the brief's 51% figure because the assessment/threshold differ.

CAMPE's Education Watch 2023 reporting indicates that in 2023, 70% of primary and 76.5% of secondary students had private tutors; the report also reports high commercial guidebook use. Before using these as load-bearing claims, extract the original report's table, denominator, sampling design and wording. Mahmud's English-tutoring figures (84.7% urban and 60.7% rural) derive from 2014–15 data and must be labelled historical and subject-specific. A Wave Foundation study reported by TBS concerned 23 government primary schools in four disaster-prone upazilas and 804 people; it is local and cannot represent national prevalence without the original design and sampling details.

## Proposed original analysis: examination-paper content analysis

This is central to the paper's title and must not be replaced by general impressions. Before coding:
- Define the target population of papers and years; select a transparent, defensible sample across SSC/HSC subjects and paper types.
- Publish inclusion/exclusion rules and obtain lawful source copies.
- Build a rubric distinguishing recall, explanation, routine application, non-routine application, analysis/evaluation, and authentic/extended response; define categories with examples.
- Code items, not merely whole papers; specify weighting by item count and marks.
- Have a second coder independently code a subset; report agreement and resolve disagreements transparently.
- Record subject, year, paper, item number, marks, response format and cognitive category in a machine-readable file.
- Report results by subject/year and sensitivity to alternative coding decisions.
- Avoid equating cognitive taxonomy labels with real-world transfer or assuming that every recall item is educationally worthless.
- If this analysis cannot be completed before the paper draft, describe it as a proposed next study and narrow the manuscript's conclusions accordingly.

## Drafting and quality assurance rules

1. Draft from the evidence matrix, not from rhetoric or a preferred conclusion.
2. Put citations directly after the claims they support; avoid citation dumps at paragraph ends when claims differ.
3. Give readers enough context to understand every statistic without opening another document.
4. Explain technical terms at first use; avoid unexplained abbreviations.
5. Use “percentage points” for differences between percentages.
6. Distinguish national estimates from local studies and convenience samples.
7. Do not infer causality from cross-sectional surveys or press coverage.
8. Include both benefits and risks of examinations, tutoring, technology and proposed reforms.
9. Treat budget, teacher workload, class size, disability inclusion, language, household cost and policy continuity as real implementation constraints.
10. Avoid implying that one country or one pedagogical technique supplies a universal solution.
11. Keep the reference list clean and complete; no internal IDs, unexplained file names or raw worklog notes.
12. The abstract, title-level conclusion and policy recommendations must be written only after evidence synthesis and gap decisions are complete.
