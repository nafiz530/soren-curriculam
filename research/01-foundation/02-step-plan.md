# Step 2 — What Is Education?

**Author:** Md. Nafiz  
**Phase:** 1 — Research Foundation  
**Status:** Research in progress — not approved  
**Official research title:** *Is our education system actually preparing students for the future, or is it mainly preparing students to pass examinations?*

## Purpose

Before deciding what an education system should accomplish, I need to clarify what education means. The word is used differently in everyday conversation, philosophy, psychology, policy, economics, and international statistics. Treating those meanings as interchangeable could lead to unclear arguments and poorly defined goals.

## Main question

> What should count as education, and how is it different from learning, schooling, instruction, training, and qualification?

## Questions to investigate

1. How do official international statistical frameworks define education and educational programmes?
2. How do philosophers distinguish education from training, socialization, or the acquisition of information?
3. What is the difference between education as a process and education as an institution or system?
4. How does education relate to learning, and can learning happen outside formal education?
5. What distinguishes schooling from education more broadly?
6. How do instruction, practice, training, experience, and self-directed study relate to education?
7. Is education primarily individual development, cultural transmission, preparation for social participation, qualification for work, or a combination?
8. Which definitions are descriptive, normative, or designed for measurement?
9. What is gained or lost when a broad concept is converted into measurable indicators?
10. Which working definitions are useful for this research, and what limitations must accompany them?

## Initial distinctions to test

These are provisional, not final definitions:

- **Learning:** change or development in knowledge, understanding, skills, attitudes, values, or behaviour through experience, practice, study, or instruction.
- **Education:** processes and arrangements intended to support learning and development; it can also refer to the institutions and social arrangements through which these aims are pursued.
- **Schooling:** organized participation in a school or comparable formal institution.
- **Instruction:** deliberate guidance or communication intended to help someone learn.
- **Training:** preparation for particular tasks, practices, or roles; it can be part of education without necessarily covering all its purposes.
- **Qualification:** formal recognition that specified requirements have been met; it is an outcome or credential, not the whole of education.

Real-world usage overlaps. These distinctions must be checked against credible literature rather than presented as universally accepted wording.

## Source strategy

Prioritize primary and scholarly sources, including UNESCO Institute for Statistics' International Standard Classification of Education (ISCED 2011), the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on philosophy of education, UNESCO material on education as a human right, and the project's archived UNESCO report, Reimagining our futures together.

Operational definitions help with consistent classification; philosophical analysis helps examine concepts and values. Neither alone settles every question.

## Evidence rules

For each significant source, record its type, relevance, definition or argument, whether the claim is descriptive/normative/empirical/operational, what it does not establish, and any credible disagreement. Do not accept a definition simply because it appears in an official framework, or treat one philosophical account as the only legitimate view.

## Planned outputs

1. A plain-language explanation of education and related concepts.
2. A comparison of operational, philosophical, and rights-based approaches.
3. A provisional glossary with source attribution and caveats.
4. A record of disagreements and unresolved questions.
5. A revised synthesis after source review and challenge.

## Approval gate

Step 2 remains a draft until I have reviewed source material, challenged the definitions, considered competing interpretations, and approved the final wording. Its purpose is to clarify the research, not to decide the final education system prematurely.
