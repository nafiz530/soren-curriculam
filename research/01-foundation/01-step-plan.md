# Step 1 — Research Question, Objectives & Scope

**Phase:** 1 — Research Foundation  
**Status:** Research in progress — not approved as final

## Purpose

Step 1 establishes exactly what this project is trying to investigate before the project begins designing an education system.

It must prevent scope drift, hidden assumptions, premature solutions, and the mistake of treating the initial question as already answered.

## Primary research question

Working formulation:

> **What should an education system look like if its purpose is to help people develop the knowledge, capabilities, judgement, character, agency, and foundations needed to learn, live, work, participate in society, and continue developing across their lives?**

The project's motivating question remains:

> **Is our education system actually preparing students for the future, or is it mainly preparing students to pass examinations?**

The second question is a motivating problem statement, not an assumption that examination-oriented education is universally ineffective.

## Step 1 research tasks

1. Examine how credible sources define or frame education.
2. Identify the purposes attributed to education by different traditions and institutions.
3. Separate normative purposes from measurable educational outcomes.
4. Identify tensions among knowledge, skills, personal development, employment, citizenship, social cohesion, culture, well-being, and lifelong learning.
5. Examine Bangladesh's stated educational purposes and policy commitments.
6. Establish the population and educational stages covered by this project.
7. Establish the geographical scope and how Bangladesh-specific evidence will interact with international evidence.
8. Define what the project means by "education system", "student", "learning", "future", "success", and related terms.
9. Define explicit exclusions so the project does not become unlimited.
10. Define intended research outputs and who should be able to understand them.
11. Identify assumptions that must be tested later rather than accepted now.
12. Produce a reviewed Step 1 scope statement that can govern later phases.

## Evidence strategy

Step 1 should prioritize:

- primary policy documents;
- major international research reports;
- peer-reviewed scholarship where relevant;
- official datasets and measurement frameworks;
- Bangladesh policy and system evidence;
- credible comparative evidence.

Institutional frameworks are useful for mapping how major organizations conceptualize education, but they are not automatically empirical proof.

## Planned source groups

### A. Purpose and future of education
- UNESCO Futures of Education
- OECD Future of Education and Skills / Learning Compass
- UNICEF education strategy

### B. Bangladesh policy
- Bangladesh National Education Policy 2010
- current official curriculum/policy documents identified during research

### C. Learning and system outcomes
- World Bank / UNESCO learning measures
- Bangladesh-specific learning evidence
- official national assessment evidence as identified

### D. Critical and alternative perspectives
Later searching must deliberately include credible work that challenges:
- competency-first approaches;
- technology-led reform;
- examination reform;
- purely economic definitions of educational success;
- purely philosophical definitions of education.

## Inclusion principles

Include a source when it:
- directly informs a Step 1 question;
- has identifiable authorship or institutional provenance;
- provides enough methodological or contextual information to evaluate;
- can be accurately cited and traced;
- adds evidence, a serious conceptual argument, a policy position, or a documented counterargument.

## Exclusion principles

Exclude or quarantine a source when it:
- makes unsupported universal claims;
- is primarily promotional without evidence;
- cannot be traced to an identifiable source;
- uses data without adequate provenance;
- is merely repeating another source without adding value;
- is being used only because it confirms an existing preference.

## Planned outputs

- approved research question;
- secondary research questions;
- research objectives;
- scope and boundaries;
- terminology/definitions;
- intended audience;
- source and evidence map;
- assumptions and open questions;
- Step 1 review record.

## Approval gate

Step 1 is complete only after:
1. sources have been reviewed;
2. the scope has been challenged;
3. competing interpretations have been recorded;
4. the wording has been revised;
5. the user approves the final version;
6. the approved version is committed as the research record.
