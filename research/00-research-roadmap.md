# Research Roadmap

## Soren Education System Research

**Author:** Md. Nafiz

### Official Research Title

> **Is our education system actually preparing students for the future, or is it mainly preparing students to pass examinations?**

This is the question the research will investigate, not a conclusion assumed in advance.

### Broader Design Question

> What should an education system look like if its purpose is to help people develop the knowledge, capabilities, judgement, character, agency, and foundations needed to learn, live, work, participate in society, and continue developing across their lives?

---

## How This Roadmap Will Be Used

This roadmap is the research architecture. It is broad enough to support a substantial project, but it is **not frozen**. A step may be split, merged, reordered, expanded, or removed when evidence and research needs justify doing so.

The project should not jump directly from criticism of existing education to a proposed replacement. The research should first understand:

**the learner → learning → history → existing systems → evidence → curriculum → teaching → assessment → teachers → technology → society → implementation → proposed system**

The final system must emerge from the research rather than being predetermined.

**Research workflow:** Research → Discuss → Review → Revise → Approve → Commit → Continue.

**Step 1 status:** I reviewed the files individually and approved the scope on 2026-10-09.

# Phase 2 — Understanding the Learner

## 5. How Humans Learn
Research learning, memory, retrieval, practice, attention, prior knowledge, feedback, cognitive load, and related mechanisms.

## 6. Child & Adolescent Development
Examine cognitive, physical, emotional, and social development across educational ages.

## 7. Motivation, Engagement & Independence
Investigate intrinsic and extrinsic motivation, engagement, autonomy, habits, self-regulation, and independent learning.

## 8. Individual Differences & Student Diversity
Examine differences in ability, prior knowledge, interests, learning needs, circumstances, pace, disability, and other factors relevant to educational design.

# Phase 3 — Understanding Existing Education

## 9. History of Formal Education
Investigate how modern schooling developed and why grades, classrooms, timetables, subjects, teachers, homework, and examinations became dominant structures.

## 10. Anatomy of the Modern Education System
Map how students, teachers, schools, families, examinations, governments, universities, employers, and other institutions interact.

## 11. Problems, Trade-offs & Unintended Consequences
Identify documented weaknesses and trade-offs in existing systems without assuming that every traditional practice is ineffective.

## 12. What Existing Systems Get Right
Identify valuable structures and practices that should be preserved, improved, or adapted rather than discarded.

# Phase 4 — Comparative & International Evidence

## 13. Comparative Education Systems
Study a carefully selected range of national and regional education systems and the conditions in which they operate.

## 14. Curriculum & School Models
Compare subject-based, competency-based, interdisciplinary, centralized, decentralized, academic, vocational, and hybrid approaches.

## 15. Assessment & Examination Models
Compare standardized examinations, coursework, projects, practical assessment, portfolios, continuous assessment, and mixed systems.

## 16. Educational Reform: Successes, Failures & Lessons
Study major reforms and identify why interventions succeeded, failed, produced limited results, or generated unintended effects.

# Phase 5 — Defining the Learner's Journey

## 17. Educational Stages & Progression
Determine how an education system could be structured from early childhood through secondary education and onward pathways.

## 18. Age, Readiness & Progression
Investigate what should determine progression: age, mastery, time, developmental readiness, or combinations of these.

## 19. Core Knowledge vs Choice
Determine what every educated person should learn and when meaningful choice and specialization should begin.

## 20. Academic, Technical & Vocational Pathways
Design principles for multiple respected pathways without creating unnecessary hierarchy between them.

# Phase 6 — Curriculum Architecture

## 21. Curriculum Design Principles
Establish what makes a curriculum coherent, age-appropriate, cumulative, manageable, relevant, and evidence-informed.

## 22. Core Subject Domains
Research and design the place of mathematics, natural sciences, languages, humanities, social sciences, computing, arts, physical education, and other foundational domains.

## 23. Practical, Interdisciplinary & Real-World Learning
Determine how projects, laboratories, experimentation, fieldwork, problem solving, and interdisciplinary learning should interact with subject knowledge.

## 24. Life Skills, Communication & Future Capabilities
Investigate financial literacy, communication, critical thinking, digital literacy, decision-making, collaboration, information literacy, and other capabilities.

# Phase 7 — Teaching & Learning Environment

## 25. Teaching Methodology
Evaluate instructional approaches and determine when different methods are appropriate rather than searching for one universal teaching method.

## 26. Classroom, Timetable, Homework & Learning Environment
Research class size, schedules, lesson structure, homework, independent study, school time, physical environments, and resource use.

## 27. Practical & Project-Based Learning
Design evidence-informed approaches for laboratories, projects, demonstrations, fieldwork, creation, experimentation, and authentic problem solving.

# Phase 8 — Assessment & Examinations

## 28. Purpose and Design of Assessment
Determine what assessment is for and what valid assessment should measure.

## 29. Formative, Summative & Practical Assessment
Examine assessment during learning, end-of-stage assessment, practical competence, projects, portfolios, demonstrations, and other approaches.

## 30. Examinations, Grades & High-Stakes Decisions
Investigate what examinations and grades can and cannot measure, including their use for progression, certification, and admission.

# Phase 9 — Teachers, Technology & AI

## 31. Teacher Role, Preparation & Professional Development
Define the teacher's responsibilities, preparation, professional learning, workload, autonomy, career progression, and accountability.

## 32. Technology in Education
Investigate when technology improves learning, when it adds little value, and how infrastructure and access affect outcomes.

## 33. Artificial Intelligence & the Future of Learning
Examine AI tutoring, personalization, feedback, accessibility, assessment, academic integrity, dependency, privacy, misinformation, and the changing role of human educators.

# Phase 10 — Equity, Governance & System Conditions

## 34. Equity, Accessibility & Student Protection
Investigate socioeconomic inequality, disability, geography, language, gender-related barriers where relevant, accessibility, safety, and protection of students.

## 35. Families, School Leadership & Governance
Examine the roles of parents, school leaders, local authorities, governments, regulators, and communities.

## 36. Resources, Funding & Low-Resource Implementation
Determine what the proposed system requires and how it could function in rural, low-resource, overcrowded, or technologically limited environments.

# Phase 11 — Education Beyond School

## 37. Higher Education, Careers & Workforce Transition
Examine how school education should connect with university, technical education, vocational training, employment, career exploration, and changing labor markets.

## 38. Citizenship, Society & Lifelong Learning
Investigate education's relationship with civic participation, social responsibility, personal development, and the ability to continue learning throughout life.

# Phase 12 — Designing, Testing & Defending the Proposed System

## 39. Complete Proposed Education System
Convert the accumulated evidence into an explicit system architecture covering educational stages, curriculum, teaching, assessment, teachers, pathways, technology, governance, and expected outcomes.

## 40. Implementation, Evaluation, Stress Testing & Final Framework
Develop implementation stages, resource considerations, measurable outcomes, evaluation methods, risks, limitations, failure scenarios, and adversarial tests. Revise the proposal based on those findings and produce the final framework.

# Research Completion Standard

The project should not be considered complete merely because all 40 steps have documents.

A major conclusion or design decision should ideally be traceable through:

**Evidence → Finding → Interpretation → Problem or Opportunity → Design Principle → Proposed Decision → Expected Outcome → Possible Failure Mode**

The final proposal should clearly distinguish:
- **Established evidence**
- **Reasonable interpretation**
- **Research-backed recommendation**
- **Design choice**
- **Hypothesis**
- **Speculation**

No proposal should be presented as established fact merely because it appears in the final system.
