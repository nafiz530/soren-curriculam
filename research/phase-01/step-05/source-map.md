# Step 5 — Source Map

**Status:** Draft discovery map, not a final appraisal.

| Source | Evidence type and scope | Claims it can support | Transfer limits / appraisal tasks |
|---|---|---|---|
| National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (2018), *How People Learn II* | Consensus report synthesizing cognitive, developmental, social, cultural, and educational research | Learning depends on interacting prior knowledge, context, motivation, development, disciplinary knowledge, feedback, and metacognition | Large synthesis; inspect relevant chapters and underlying evidence for specific claims. Not Bangladesh-specific. |
| Dunlosky et al. (2013), “Improving Students' Learning With Effective Learning Techniques” | Review of ten learning techniques across conditions, learners, materials, and outcomes | Practice testing and distributed practice received high-utility ratings in the review; other techniques vary by evidence and condition | Review is older; utility ratings reflect literature available then. Do not translate into universal ranking without examining newer evidence and task-specific conditions. |
| Weinstein, Madan & Sumeracki (2018), “Teaching the science of learning” | Tutorial review of six cognitive strategies | Identifies evidence-backed strategies and implementation implications | Review/tutorial, not one experiment; check whether each strategy applies to the desired age, subject, and outcome. |
| Carpenter, Pan & Butler (2022), “The science of effective learning with spacing and retrieval practice” | Review of spacing, retrieval, and metacognition | Supports investigation of retrieval and spacing as learning strategies | Does not imply every quiz or interval is effective; examine study conditions and outcome types. |
| Distributed-practice classroom review (2025) | Systematic review/meta-analysis: 22 reports, 31 effect sizes, N > 3,000 | Average benefit of distributed over massed practice in included classroom studies (d = 0.54; 95% CI 0.31–0.77) | Moderator analysis limited; reported retention intervals did not exceed 42 days; exact applicability to Bangladesh remains unknown. |
| Freeman et al. (2014), “Active learning increases student performance in science, engineering, and mathematics” | Meta-analysis of undergraduate STEM studies | Active learning improved examination performance and reduced failure relative to traditional lecturing in included studies | Undergraduate STEM context; “active learning” covers varied interventions; not a direct estimate for Bangladeshi secondary schools. |

## Claim-relative appraisal questions

For every major learning claim, record:
- Construct and outcome: acquisition, immediate performance, retention, understanding, transfer, fluency, or self-regulation.
- Learner population, age, prior knowledge, language, and setting.
- Subject/material and intervention details.
- Comparison condition, duration, delay to assessment, and time-on-task if available.
- Study design, sampling, risk of bias, funding/conflicts, and publication/language bias.
- Whether results replicate across independent lines of evidence.
- Counterevidence and the conditions in which the strategy may not help.
- Transferability to Bangladesh and the specific conditions needed for the mechanism to operate.

## Coverage gaps

- No dedicated Bangla-language search has yet been completed.
- Bangladesh-specific classroom studies have not yet been systematically searched.
- Full primary-study appraisal is pending.
- The source set is discovery-level, not exhaustive and not a systematic review.
