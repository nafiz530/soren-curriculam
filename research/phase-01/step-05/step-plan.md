# Step 5 — How Humans Learn: Research Plan

**Author:** Md. Nafiz  
**Status:** DRAFT — awaiting review and approval  
**Roadmap position:** Phase 2, Step 5  
**Method standard:** Approved Step 4 protocol

## Research question

What does reliable evidence tell us about how people learn, remember, understand, transfer knowledge, develop skills, and regulate their own learning—and what can education reasonably do with that evidence?

This step is not a hunt for one universal teaching method. It should distinguish mechanisms, outcomes, learner differences, subject domains, age groups, and classroom conditions.

## Scope

Investigate:
1. What counts as learning: short-term performance, retention, understanding, transfer, procedural fluency, conceptual change, and independent use.
2. Prior knowledge, knowledge organization, memory, attention, and working-memory constraints.
3. Practice, retrieval, spacing, interleaving, elaboration, worked examples, and feedback.
4. Understanding, reasoning, transfer, problem-solving, and disciplinary knowledge.
5. Motivation, emotion, metacognition, self-regulation, language, culture, and social context.
6. Development across childhood, adolescence, and adulthood; avoid assuming age-independent effects.
7. Differences between laboratory findings and classroom learning, and between subjects and assessment types.
8. How learning evidence is misrepresented in popular education claims.
9. Bangladesh-specific applicability, including language, class size, curriculum, access to materials, teacher workload, tutoring, and exam incentives.

## Method and artifacts

- Search and screen reviews, consensus reports, meta-analyses, strong classroom studies, and relevant Bangladesh evidence.
- Record exact queries and coverage in a search log.
- Map each major claim to its source, construct, population, outcome, design, transfer limits, and counterevidence.
- Distinguish established findings from plausible interpretations and design implications.
- Conduct a devil's-advocate pass before accepting high-impact conclusions.
- Do not infer that a strategy improves deep understanding or transfer merely because it improves recall or a near-transfer test.
- Do not treat a research-supported technique as a complete teaching philosophy or as a substitute for content knowledge, teachers, resources, and context.

## Initial source set

- National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (2018), *How People Learn II: Learners, Contexts, and Cultures*.
- Dunlosky et al. (2013), review of ten learning techniques.
- Weinstein, Madan, and Sumeracki (2018), tutorial review of the science of learning.
- Carpenter, Pan, and Butler (2022), review of spacing and retrieval practice.
- Freeman et al. (2014), meta-analysis of active learning in undergraduate STEM.
- Classroom-applied meta-analysis of distributed practice (2025 publication).
- Bangladesh-specific studies to be located in the next search pass.
