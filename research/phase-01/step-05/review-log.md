## Initial adversarial pass

### What the draft currently does well
- Distinguishes immediate performance from retention, understanding, transfer, fluency, and self-regulation.
- Uses consensus and review literature rather than presenting isolated findings as universal truths.
- Identifies the populations and subject domains that constrain transfer from major studies.
- Avoids treating retrieval practice as an argument for high-stakes testing or assuming one pedagogy is universally best.
- Names Bangladesh-specific search gaps and implementation conditions.

### Counterarguments and cautions
1. The evidence for retrieval and spacing is strong in many contexts, but effect sizes vary and do not guarantee transfer or deep understanding.
2. The 2013 learning-techniques review is useful but not current enough to stand alone; newer reviews and primary studies must be considered.
3. Active-learning evidence cited here is about undergraduate STEM, not Bangladesh secondary education generally.
4. A consensus report is broad and valuable, but it does not make every statement equally well supported; claims must be tied to specific chapters and underlying studies.
5. The current source set is mostly English-language and international. Without Bangla and Bangladesh-specific evidence, contextual claims remain hypotheses.
6. Learning is not only cognitive strategy. Instructional quality, subject knowledge, time, resources, language, disability support, and teacher working conditions may constrain implementation.
7. Popular labels such as “learning styles,” “active learning,” and “critical thinking” can be used imprecisely; define constructs before drawing policy implications.
