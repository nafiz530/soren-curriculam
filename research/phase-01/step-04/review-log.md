## Current methodological decisions

| Decision | Rationale | Limitation / application rule |
|---|---|---|
| Describe the project as a structured, transparent, critical evidence synthesis unless a specific review meets systematic-review requirements | Avoids claiming exhaustive coverage that has not been demonstrated | Reassess per question; do not use the label “systematic review” casually |
| Appraise sources relative to the claim they support | Provenance and prestige are not universal verdicts on truth | Record what a source can and cannot establish |
| Define constructs before title-level synthesis | Similar labels can conceal incompatible outcomes | Record construct, measure, setting and match for each source |
| Use a claim-relative “established evidence” threshold | Avoids an arbitrary universal evidence hierarchy | Must search for counterevidence and check independence and scope |
| Require a logged devil's-advocate pass for high-impact claims | Reduces the risk of selecting evidence to justify a preferred proposal | Cannot remove bias; invite human challenge where feasible |
| Record funding, commissioning, language, and publication risks | These can shape questions, visibility, and reported results | Funding alone does not invalidate research |
| Use full traceability for load-bearing claims and lighter records for background | Keeps accountability proportional and usable | Upgrade a background claim before it becomes load-bearing |
| Treat incompatible measures separately | Prevents false precision from pooling unlike outcomes | Measurement incompatibility may itself be a field-level finding |
| Resolve normative disagreements through explicit value reasoning | Evidence alone cannot choose educational priorities | State value premises, counterarguments, weights, and Step 3 criteria |
