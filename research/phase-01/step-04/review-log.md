## Review objective

Assess whether the protocol is transparent, feasible for a student-led project, suitable for different research question types, careful about causal claims and transferability, and explicit about ethics and uncertainty.

## Current methodological decisions

| Decision | Rationale | Limitation / question |
|---|---|---|
| Use the term “structured, transparent, critical evidence synthesis” for the overall project at this stage. | The project is planned to be systematic in its reasoning, but exhaustive search and formal screening have not been demonstrated. | Reassess if a later topic receives a fully documented systematic review. |
| Judge sources relative to the claim they support. | Official status, peer review, theoretical influence, or popularity does not establish every kind of claim. | Need practical examples as the project handles more empirical research. |
| Match methods to conceptual, normative, descriptive, causal, historical, comparative, and implementation questions. | Different questions require different kinds of evidence and inference. | Some questions span categories; the protocol must permit mixed methods. |
| Treat triangulation as critical comparison, not source-counting. | Sources may be dependent, incompatible, or methodologically weak. | Need careful documentation of source independence and disagreements. |
| Treat Bangladesh as the primary contextual case. | The research is intended to inform an education system relevant to Bangladesh. | Local evidence may be incomplete or hard to access; gaps must remain visible. |
| Include ethical safeguards before any future primary data collection. | The research may eventually concern children, teachers, families, or sensitive experiences. | Local legal, school, guardian, and institutional requirements must be checked before collection. |

## Source limitations

- PRISMA is a reporting guideline for systematic reviews, not a general quality score or proof of completeness.
- WWC has a defined remit and standards; its procedures should not be imposed mechanically on all source types or contexts.
- The Magenta Book is UK government evaluation guidance; methodological ideas require contextual judgement.
- AERA's ethics code is professional guidance and does not replace local law or approvals.

## Open questions for author review

1. Is the protocol clear enough to use during later research without creating unnecessary bureaucracy?
2. Does it distinguish evidence, finding, interpretation, normative argument, recommendation, design choice, hypothesis, and speculation?
3. Is the distinction between structured evidence synthesis and a systematic review accurate and appropriately cautious?
4. Are quantitative, qualitative, causal, historical, philosophical, and comparative questions treated fairly?
5. Are source credibility and evidence strength correctly treated as claim-relative rather than one universal hierarchy?
6. Are Bangladesh-specific transferability and language issues sufficiently explicit?
7. Are ethics safeguards appropriate before any future data collection involving people or minors?
8. Which corrections are necessary before approval?
