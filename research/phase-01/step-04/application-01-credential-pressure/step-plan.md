## Research question

Why does examination pressure persist in Bangladesh, and what roles do learning, certification, selection/ranking, credential competition, labor-market signaling, institutional accountability, and supplementary tutoring play?

The question is open. The investigation must not assume that examinations are useless, that all tutoring is harmful, or that signaling is the only reason credentials matter.

## Subquestions

1. What distinct functions do examinations and qualifications serve: measuring learning, certifying achievement, selecting/ranking candidates, and signaling to institutions or employers?
2. What Bangladesh-specific evidence links SSC/HSC assessment to classroom practice and teaching-to-the-test?
3. What roles do schools, families, teachers, admissions competition, employment structures, and supplementary tutoring play?
4. Which students gain support from tutoring, and who faces cost or access disadvantages?
5. What evidence would distinguish a problem caused by test design from one caused by the wider incentive system?
6. What can the evidence establish now, and what requires primary policy records, representative data, or stronger causal research?

## Working construct definitions for this pilot

**Examination pressure** is a set of observable mechanisms rather than a single score: reported pressure from parents, schools, or institutions to achieve grades; teaching and learning narrowly aligned with expected test formats; the role of SSC/HSC results in progression or admission; supplementary tutoring specifically for exam preparation; and reported time, financial, or opportunity costs. These indicators are not interchangeable.

**Preparation for the future** is multidimensional: durable knowledge, transfer to unfamiliar problems, subject competence, communication, judgement, learner agency, social participation, and readiness for further learning, work, and life. The selected washback and tutoring sources do not adequately measure this full construct. They can illuminate exam pressure but cannot directly prove that the system prepares students mainly for exams rather than for the future.

**Credential competition** means competition for scarce or valued opportunities in which formal qualifications or examination results affect access. **Selection** means ranking or allocating candidates to limited opportunities. **Signaling** means a credential conveys information about an applicant when a decision-maker cannot directly observe relevant capability. **Certification** means formally attesting to achievement or completion. These functions can overlap but must be coded separately.

## Scope and method

Use a structured, transparent, critical evidence synthesis. Begin with Dore (SRC-012), Spence (SRC-014), educational testing scholarship (SRC-034), Bangladesh-specific empirical studies (SRC-035–039), and primary Bangladesh policy records when policy or institutional rules are at issue. Trace statistics to original data before using them as load-bearing evidence.

The current search is focused discovery, not an exhaustive systematic review. Inspect full texts before making strong claims. Search Bangla and English in subsequent passes, including official NCTB/Ministry records, BANBEIS, CAMPE/Education Watch, BanglaJOL, and university repositories.

## Inclusion and exclusion rules

**Include:** research directly examining examination consequences, credential incentives, SSC/HSC classroom practice, supplementary tutoring, tutoring inequality, or certification/signaling mechanisms; primary official records for formal rules.

**Do not use as direct causal support:** opinion pieces without evidence; untraceable or undated statistics; foreign findings treated as direct Bangladesh evidence without transferability analysis; studies of different populations treated as equivalent; or policy statements used as proof of implementation or outcomes.

Older sources may inform theory or history, but must not be presented as current prevalence evidence.
