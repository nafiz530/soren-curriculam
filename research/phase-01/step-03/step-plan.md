# Step 3 — What Is Education For?

**Author:** Md. Nafiz  
**Phase:** 1 — Research Foundation  
**Status:** Draft — review pending  
**Official research title:** *Is our education system actually preparing students for the future, or is it mainly preparing students to pass examinations?*

## Purpose and main question

Step 2 established a provisional working definition of education. This step examines the purposes that may justify education and the difficult choices that arise when those purposes compete. It must not merely produce an attractive list of aims: it must investigate who chooses them, why they are justified, how they relate, and what evidence could show whether an education system serves them.

> What purposes should education serve for learners and society, and how should an education system reason transparently about conflicts among those purposes?

## Questions to investigate

1. What intrinsic, instrumental, relational, and collective goods can education provide?
2. How do knowledge, personal development, livelihood, citizenship, culture, equality, well-being, economic development, and lifelong learning relate?
3. Which purposes are widely endorsed, which are contested, and which depend on political or cultural values?
4. How can aims be justified without reducing education to employment, test scores, national economic output, or a vague promise of “holistic development”?
5. Which responsibilities belong to education institutions, and which are shared with families, communities, governments, employers, and others?
6. How should aims account for rights, learner agency, pluralism, inequality, uncertainty, and future generations?
7. What would count as evidence of progress, and what would remain difficult to measure?
8. How should the aims be interpreted in Bangladesh, and what limits apply when using international frameworks?
9. What are the strongest counterarguments to the major purposes identified?
10. What provisional criteria should a later proposed system satisfy without designing that system prematurely?

## Working categories to test

These are a map for investigation, not an approved final list or ranking:

- **Knowledge and understanding:** worthwhile knowledge, disciplinary understanding, intellectual curiosity, and reasoning.
- **Personal development and agency:** judgement, autonomy appropriate to age, self-understanding, interests, and responsibility.
- **Livelihood and economic participation:** work preparation, occupational learning, adaptability, and economic independence.
- **Citizenship and collective life:** public understanding, participation, cooperation, and responsibility towards others.
- **Culture and continuity:** language, history, cultural knowledge, identity, intergenerational learning, and critical examination of inherited practices.
- **Equity and human rights:** meaningful opportunity, inclusion, dignity, and the conditions needed to exercise rights.
- **Well-being and flourishing:** physical, emotional, relational, creative, and intellectual dimensions of living well.
- **Collective and planetary futures:** shared problems, sustainability, peace, and future generations.

## Research method

1. Use the scholarly overview of educational aims to identify debates and trace important arguments to original works where feasible.
2. Compare normative sources (what education ought to serve), policy frameworks (what institutions commit to), and empirical evidence (what arrangements achieve, for whom, and under what conditions).
3. Read Bangladesh's National Education Policy 2010 as a statement of national policy aims, not proof that those aims have been achieved.
4. Treat international frameworks as proposals and organizing concepts, not neutral scientific rankings of what matters.
5. Seek counterarguments and risks: instrumentalizing learners, elitism, indoctrination, cultural exclusion, economic reductionism, paternalism, and vague or unmeasurable goals.
6. Keep source-derived claims, interpretation, value judgements, and proposed criteria separate.

## Inclusion criteria

- Primary education policies and frameworks with clear authorship and date.
- Scholarly work on the nature, justification, or prioritization of educational aims.
- Evidence that distinguishes stated aims from outcomes or reveals trade-offs.
- Bangladesh-specific sources relevant to national purposes and context.
- Counterarguments that challenge assumptions or reveal risks.

## Cautions and exclusions

Avoid inspirational lists of “21st-century skills” without a clear rationale; claims that one aim is the only legitimate purpose without examining alternatives; policy statements treated as proof of impact; cross-country rankings used instead of contextual analysis; predictions about future jobs presented as certain; and recommendations whose value assumptions are hidden.
