# Step 3 — What Is Education For?

**Author:** Md. Nafiz  
**Phase:** 1 — Research Foundation  
**Status:** Revised draft — review pending  
**Official research title:** *Is our education system actually preparing students for the future, or is it mainly preparing students to pass examinations?*

## Purpose and main question

Step 2 established a provisional working definition of education. This step examines the purposes that may justify education and the difficult choices that arise when those purposes compete. It must not merely produce an attractive list of aims: it must investigate who chooses them, why they are justified, how they relate, and what evidence could show whether an education system serves them.

> What purposes should education serve for learners and society, and how should an education system reason transparently about conflicts among those purposes?

## Questions to investigate

1. What intrinsic, instrumental, constitutive, relational, and collective goods can education provide?
2. How do knowledge, personal development, livelihood, citizenship, culture, equality, well-being, economic development, and lifelong learning relate?
3. What functions do certification, selection, and signaling perform, and how can exam pressure become a positional competition?
4. Which purposes are widely endorsed, which are contested, and which depend on political or cultural values?
5. How can aims be justified without reducing education to employment, test scores, national economic output, or a vague promise of “holistic development”?
6. Which responsibilities belong to education institutions, and which are shared with families, communities, governments, employers, and others?
7. How should aims account for rights, learner agency, pluralism, inequality, climate, uncertainty, and future generations?
8. What does Bangladesh's National Education Policy 2010 state, and how do its aims relate to later curriculum changes and actual outcomes?
9. How should historical integrity and core educational commitments be protected across political transitions without blocking evidence-based reform?
10. What is documented about the 2012 curriculum revision, the 2021/2022 framework, the progressive rollout from 2023, later revisions, specialized reform committees, and the reported education-reform-commission proposal?
11. Who should decide educational aims and curriculum content, who must be consulted, and how should disputes be reviewed?
12. What would count as evidence of progress, and what would remain difficult to measure?
13. What are the strongest counterarguments to the major purposes identified?
14. What provisional criteria should a later proposed system satisfy without designing that system prematurely?

## Working categories to test

These remain a map for investigation, not an approved final list or ranking:

- **Knowledge and understanding:** worthwhile knowledge, disciplinary understanding, intellectual curiosity, and reasoning.
- **Personal development and agency:** judgement, autonomy appropriate to age, self-understanding, interests, and responsibility.
- **Livelihood and economic participation:** work preparation, occupational learning, adaptability, and economic independence.
- **Certification, selection, and signaling:** credible demonstration of achievement and fair allocation of scarce opportunities.
- **Citizenship and collective life:** public understanding, participation, cooperation, and responsibility towards others.
- **Culture and continuity:** language, history, cultural knowledge, identity, intergenerational learning, and critical examination of inherited practices.
- **Equity and human rights:** meaningful opportunity, inclusion, dignity, and the conditions needed to exercise rights.
- **Well-being and flourishing:** physical, emotional, relational, creative, and intellectual dimensions of living well.
- **Collective and planetary futures:** shared problems, climate and ecological understanding, sustainability, peace, and future generations.
- **Moral and spiritual development:** values and ethical reflection, considered alongside pluralism, learner rights, and risks of indoctrination.

## Research method

1. Use the scholarly overview of educational aims to identify debates and trace important arguments to original works where feasible.
2. Engage directly with Ronald Dore on credential competition, Gert Biesta on qualification/socialization/subjectification, and Michael Spence on signaling; treat each as an argument to evaluate, not a universal law.
3. Compare normative sources (what education ought to serve), policy frameworks (what institutions commit to), and empirical evidence (what arrangements achieve, for whom, and under what conditions).
4. Read Bangladesh's National Education Policy 2010 as a statement of national policy aims, not proof that those aims have been achieved. Map its aims to the working categories and verify exact wording against the primary document.
5. Distinguish policy, curriculum framework, syllabus, textbook, assessment rules, political announcements, and formal decisions. Date the status of each claim.
6. For the post-2024 reform process, distinguish a broad national education reform commission from specialized advisory/review committees. Use committee reports and official notices where available; do not equate a reported intention with formal establishment.
7. Seek counterarguments and risks: instrumentalizing learners, elitism, indoctrination, cultural exclusion, economic reductionism, paternalism, partisan rewriting, and vague or unmeasurable goals.
8. Keep source-derived claims, interpretation, value judgements, and proposed criteria separate.

## Inclusion criteria

- Primary education policies, curriculum frameworks, syllabi, assessment rules, and official notices with clear authorship and date.
- Scholarly work on the nature, justification, prioritization, and political governance of educational aims.
- Evidence that distinguishes stated aims from outcomes or reveals trade-offs.
- Bangladesh-specific sources relevant to national purposes, curriculum history, examination pressure, and the diversity of pathways.
- Counterarguments that challenge assumptions or reveal risks.

## Cautions and exclusions

Avoid inspirational lists of “21st-century skills” without a clear rationale; claims that one aim is the only legitimate purpose without examining alternatives; policy statements treated as proof of impact; cross-country rankings used instead of contextual analysis; predictions about future jobs presented as certain; and recommendations whose value assumptions are hidden. Do not infer partisan intent from the timing of a reform alone. Do not claim an education commission was formally constituted without primary documentary evidence.

## Provisional commitments to scrutinize

- Basic rights and dignity should operate as constraints on educational design, not simply as benefits to trade away for efficiency.
- Any proposed alternative to high-stakes examinations must explain how trustworthy certification and fair allocation of opportunities will be preserved or improved.
- History and core educational commitments should not be rewritten merely to signal a change of political party; evidence-based revision must remain possible and transparent.

These are provisional commitments for examination, not a complete hierarchy of educational purposes or an approved final system.
