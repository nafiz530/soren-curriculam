# DEC-004-003 — Run a Credential-Pressure Pilot Before Step 5

**Date:** 2026-10-09  
**Status:** Provisional workflow decision; no roadmap renumbering  
**Owner:** Md. Nafiz

## Decision

After approval of Step 4, run one bounded methodology application on credential competition and examination pressure in Bangladesh before the numbered roadmap's Step 5, “How Humans Learn.” This application remains nested under Step 4 and does not replace, renumber, or mark Step 5 complete.

## Rationale

Step 3 left credential competition, SSC/HSC washback, supplementary tutoring, and pathway inequality as explicit research obligations. A concrete application tests whether the approved Step 4 method can separate theory from local evidence, distinguish certification/selection/signaling from learning, trace statistics, and preserve counterevidence before the project advances into learning science.

## Alternatives considered

- **Proceed directly to Step 5:** preserves strict sequence but postpones testing the method on an open question that exercises the new rules.
- **Renumber or replace Step 5:** rejected because it would silently alter the approved roadmap and obscure the planned learning-science stage.
- **Run a bounded application within Step 4:** selected because it tests the method while preserving roadmap numbering and obligations.

## Constraints

- This is not a conclusion that examinations are harmful or that credentials primarily signal ability.
- The application cannot be treated as a full systematic review.
- Bangladesh-specific claims must not be inferred from Dore or Spence alone.
- Statistics must be traced to original sources before becoming load-bearing.
- The application remains a draft for author discussion and review.
- Step 5 remains the next numbered roadmap step after this pilot is reviewed.

## Expected outcome

A transparent pilot demonstrating claim-relative appraisal, source-to-claim mapping, counterevidence, uncertainty, and a recorded devil's-advocate pass.

## Failure mode and revisit condition

The pilot fails if it turns a small number of qualitative studies into national conclusions, imports theory as local fact, or uses abstract-level statistics without verification. Revisit sequencing if the application becomes too broad or delays Step 5 without improving the evidence method.
